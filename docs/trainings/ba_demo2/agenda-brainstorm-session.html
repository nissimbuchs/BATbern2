Technique 1: Morphological Analysis

Systematically mapping 6 agenda items × 5 interaction dimensions

Agent Sets Up the Matrix

The agent creates a two-axis grid:

Rows — 6 Agenda Items: Greeting, Analytics, Audit Sign-off, Budget/Fee, Topics, Open Discussion

Columns — 5 Dimensions: PRE (before meeting), LIVE (during), POST (after), ARTIFACT (what it produces), NOTIFICATION (alerts/triggers)

Then begins walking through the grid cell by cell, starting at A1 × PRE with seed ideas and asking for the user's input.

Training Note

Key learning: The agent doesn't just dump 30 ideas at once. It works one cell at a time, offers seed ideas, and waits for your reaction. This is collaborative facilitation — you shape the output. Your responses steer which ideas get developed and which get discarded.

Key Takeaways for Business Analysts

What to remember when brainstorming with the BMAD Analyst Agent

🎯 Give Rich Context

Describe what exists today in detail — the agent maps the gap between "what is" and "what could be." Mention existing entities, roles, pages, and workflows. The richer the input, the more targeted the ideas.

✂️ Steer and Reject Freely

You're the domain expert. Reject over-engineered ideas ("no welcome display, no live checkin"). Say "none of these" to an entire batch. The agent adapts. Don't feel obligated to accept suggestions that don't fit your real-world constraints.

🔍 Watch for Cross-Cutting Patterns

The biggest discovery in this session — meeting minutes as a living document — emerged from a casual remark about attendance. Cross-cutting artifacts often hide in conversations about individual features. The agent helps surface them.

🎭 Role Playing Finds Edge Cases

Stand-in delegation, absent partner voting, new partner onboarding — none of these emerged from feature brainstorming. They came from being those stakeholders. When the agent asks "what would [persona] need?" — really inhabit that role.

🚧 Real-World Constraints Are Features

"We create the report on meeting day" killed 3 pre-meeting features — and that's good. Constraints shape the system toward what's actually usable. Don't hide your limitations; they're design inputs.

📊 AI-Recommended Techniques Work

The AI chose Morphological Analysis (systematic grid), Role Playing (stakeholder perspectives), and Cross-Pollination (external patterns). Each technique built on the previous one's output. Trust the technique selection — it's matched to your problem shape.

📄 The Artifact Builds Itself

The session document grew progressively — every idea captured with a structured format (ID, title, concept, novelty). By the end, organization was just grouping what already existed. No separate "write-up" phase needed.

🔗 It Feeds the Next Phase

The brainstorming output is structured for consumption by other BMAD agents. The UX Designer reads the feature inventory, the Architect reads the emerged entities, the PM reads the priorities. Each phase builds on the last.
