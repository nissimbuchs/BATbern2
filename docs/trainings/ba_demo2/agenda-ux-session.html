Step 2 — Project Understanding
Sally — Synthesis

After loading all documents, Sally synthesized a comprehensive project understanding:

5 Target Users Identified:

UserPrimary Need
OrganizerRun the meeting efficiently, produce minutes as a byproduct
Present PartnerFollow the agenda, vote, feel informed and included

New PartnerUnderstand the platform and community before first meeting

Phase 2.2: Core Design

Steps 3-5 — Core experience, emotional journey, and inspiration analysis

Step 3 — Core Experience
Sally — Core Experience Definition

The One-Line Vision:

"Walk through the agenda, and the meeting documents itself."

The Presenter Mode is THE critical interaction. The organizer navigates a slide-like interface. Each agenda item is a "slide" with built-in interactions (vote, upload viewer, notes). As the organizer presents each item, the meeting minutes assemble automatically in the background.

5 Experience Principles:

PrincipleMeaning
One Flow, One ScreenLinear agenda progression. No branching, no deep navigation.
Capture by UsingEvery interaction (vote, note, upload) automatically feeds into minutes.
Projector-Readable AlwaysMinimum 24px type, high contrast, no fine details.
Twice-a-Year ObviousZero training needed. Self-evident controls.
Meeting Extends Beyond RoomAbsent partners, async votes, published minutes — the meeting isn't over when the projector turns off.

Step 7 — Defining Experience
Sally — Experience Mechanics

Mental Model: "PowerPoint slide, but interactive." Each agenda item is a full-screen "slide" with built-in interaction areas. The organizer advances linearly, just like a presentation.

Success Criteria:

  • 90%+ of meeting minutes content captured automatically
  • Zero training needed for organizer (self-evident controls)
  • 30-second maximum for any vote sequence

Novel Patterns:

  • Minutes as Byproduct — attendance check, vote results, notes all flow into minutes without explicit "save to minutes" actions
  • Proxied Voting — organizer casts on behalf of stand-ins with clear attribution chain

Experience Mechanics Flow: Initiation (Start Meeting button) → Per-item interactions (present, discuss, vote/note) → Feedback loop (result shown on projector) → Completion (all items done, minutes preview).

Step 13 — Responsive & Accessibility
Sally — Responsive & Accessibility Strategy

Dual Responsive Strategy:

  • Presenter Mode = Desktop Fixed — No responsive breakpoints. Designed for 1920x1080 projector. Sidebar collapses only below 1280px (laptop fallback).
  • Partner Portal = Full Responsive — Standard MUI breakpoints (xs/sm/md/lg/xl). Mobile-first for absent partner catch-up on phone.

WCAG 2.1 AA Compliance:

  • 4.5:1 contrast ratio minimum (7:1 for projector text)
  • Full keyboard navigation in presenter mode
  • Screen reader support with ARIA live regions for vote results
  • Focus management: auto-focus on active agenda item

Device Testing Matrix: Projector (1920x1080), Laptop (1366x768 fallback), Tablet (partner portal), Mobile (partner portal catch-up).

Key Takeaways for Business Analysts

What to remember when running the UX Design workflow with BMAD

➡️ Let the Workflow Guide You

Typing c moves you through the 14 steps in order. The workflow enforces the right sequence — understanding before design, principles before pixels, journeys before components. Trust the order.

🎯 Your Domain Expertise Matters Most

The agent asked only 3 clarifying questions in Step 2, but those answers ("projector-first, no devices, 2x/year") shaped every subsequent design decision. Your domain knowledge is the critical input — the agent is the structured process.

♻️ Garbage In, Garbage Out

The agent builds on YOUR answers. When asked "what apps do your users love?" — naming PowerPoint led to the slide-based presenter mode metaphor. Naming Mentimeter led to projector-first voting. The more specific your input, the more tailored the output.

👀 Design Directions Let You SEE

Step 9 produced an interactive HTML file with 6 layout options you can click through at full screen. This is where design decisions become tangible. Don't just pick one — compose from multiple directions like the user did (A + E + F).

📋 The Spec Is Implementation-Ready

The final ux-design-specification.md includes component specs, accessibility requirements, responsive strategy, and user journeys with edge cases. Hand it to developers directly — no separate "translation" step needed.

🔗 It Feeds the Next Phase

Just like brainstorming feeds UX, the UX specification feeds Architecture and PRD. The Architect Agent reads the component list and user journeys to design APIs and entities. The PM Agent reads the journeys to create epics and stories. Each phase builds on the last.
