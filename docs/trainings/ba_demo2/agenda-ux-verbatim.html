Executive Summary

Project Vision

Transform BATbern's bi-annual partner meetings from manual, verbal processes into an interactive, system-driven experience. Six static agenda items become live features with pre-meeting preparation, real-time meeting execution via a projector-driven presenter mode, and post-meeting async workflows. The system captures meeting minutes as a natural byproduct of use — no one "takes notes," the meeting documents itself.

Target Users

Primary: Organizer (Meeting Driver)

  • Runs the meeting from their laptop, projected for all partners to see
  • Needs a streamlined controller interface: advance agenda items, trigger votes, upload documents, take notes
  • Tech-savvy, but uses this only twice a year — interface must be self-explanatory
  • Success = running the meeting without ever leaving the system

Secondary: Present Partner (In-Room Participant)

  • Views the projected screen — no personal device interaction during the meeting
  • Participates verbally; organizer captures their input (votes, attendance)
  • Needs to see clear, readable content on the projector (large text, high contrast)

Secondary: Absent Partner (Async Participant)

  • Receives meeting minutes and participates in post-meeting workflows via the partner portal
  • Needs complete context: what was presented, what was decided, what requires their action
  • Interacts via desktop or mobile for fee acceptance, feedback pulse

Tertiary: New Partner (First-Year Onboarding)

  • First exposure to the BATbern partner meeting system
  • Needs to understand the community and process quickly — onboarding tour, context package
  • Visible "New" badge in the Who Is Who gallery signals veterans to be welcoming

Tertiary: Stand-In (Non-System Delegate)

  • Attends on behalf of a partner company without a system account
  • Organizer records their presence and proxies their votes with clear attribution
  • No login required — represented via free-text name in the system

Key Design Challenges

  1. Projector-First Design — The primary consumption mode during the meeting is a projector. This means large typography, high contrast, minimal clutter, and no interactions that require individual partner devices. The organizer's laptop is the sole input device.
  1. Bi-Annual Usage = Zero Learning Curve — Partners see this system twice a year. The organizer uses it twice a year. Every screen must be immediately understandable without training or memory of last time. Strong visual affordances, obvious next actions, no hidden features.
  1. Minutes as Byproduct — Every organizer action (toggling attendance, triggering a vote, uploading a document, typing a note) must silently contribute to auto-assembling meeting minutes. The UX challenge: make this invisible during the meeting, reviewable after.
  1. Async Continuity — Post-meeting workflows (fee acceptance, feedback, action item review) must feel like a natural extension of the meeting, not a disconnected system. Absent partners need enough context to participate meaningfully.
  1. Stand-In Governance — Proxy voting and delegation must have clear attribution chains without adding complexity. The organizer is the sole operator — stand-in representation must be quick and unambiguous.

Design Opportunities

  1. "The Meeting Runs Itself" — A well-designed presenter mode where the organizer walks through the agenda and the system captures everything creates an experience that feels magical. No note-taker, no separate spreadsheet, no "can someone email the PDF" — it just works.
  1. Reusable Interaction Vocabulary — Four components (vote, document viewer, note-taker, attendance gallery) recur across all agenda items. Consistent patterns mean partners instantly recognize "oh, this is a vote" regardless of whether it's audit sign-off or budget approval.
  1. Institutional Memory — Year-over-year meeting archives with analytics snapshots, decision records, and action item history build a governance asset. "What did we decide about fees in 2024?" becomes a 10-second lookup.
  1. Projector-Optimized Dark Mode — The existing BATbern dark theme (zinc-950 + blue-400) is perfect for projector readability. This becomes a distinctive visual identity for partner meetings — professional, high-contrast, unmistakably "meeting mode."

Inspiring Products Analysis

1. OneNote — Meeting Minutes

What it does well:

  • Freeform note-taking with structure — You can type anywhere, but sections and pages give it organization. Notes feel effortless because there's no rigid template forcing you into a format.
  • Auto-save is invisible — You never think about saving. It just happens. This is exactly the "Capture by Using" principle.
  • Sections as agenda items — Each section tab maps naturally to a meeting topic. You click the tab, you're in that topic's notes.
  • Rich content embedding — Paste images, attach files, embed tables — all inline. The document becomes a living artifact.

What we should take:

  • The mental model of "sections = agenda items" maps directly to our agenda sidebar
  • Invisible auto-save — no save button, no "saving..." spinner, just a subtle checkmark
  • The ability to mix structured content (vote results, attendance) with free-form notes in the same space

What to avoid:

  • OneNote's freeform canvas can be messy — our minutes need more structure since they auto-assemble
  • The navigation can get lost in deep hierarchies — we need flat, linear agenda progression

2. Miro — PI Planning Days (SAFe)

What it does well:

  • The "infinite board" feeling — Everything is visible at once, spatially organized. For PI planning, teams see all their work laid out.
  • Facilitated sessions — Miro has a facilitator mode where one person drives what everyone sees. This is exactly our presenter mode concept.
  • Voting/dot voting — Built-in voting mechanics where people place dots on items. Simple, visual, instant results.
  • Timer + facilitation tools — Countdown timers, spotlight features, "bring everyone to me" — tools for the person running the session.

What we should take:

  • Facilitator/presenter mode pattern — The organizer controls the view; everyone follows. Miro proved this works for group sessions.
  • Built-in voting — Miro's dot voting is instantly understood. Our approve/decline is even simpler, but the visual clarity of "8 green dots = approved" is the right pattern.
  • Agenda as a spatial journey — In Miro PI planning, you move from board to board. Our agenda items are the same concept: move from item to item.

What to avoid:

  • Miro's complexity — it's a general-purpose tool with a massive learning curve. We need purpose-built simplicity.
  • The "too much on screen" problem — Miro can overwhelm. Our projector-first design demands focus on one thing at a time.

3. Microsoft PowerPoint — Presenter Mode

What it does well:

  • Presenter view vs. audience view — The presenter sees notes, next slide, timer. The audience sees only the slide. This dual-view model is exactly our organizer laptop vs. projector concept.
  • Linear slide progression — Click to advance. Everyone knows how it works. Zero learning curve.
  • Slide transitions feel smooth — Moving between slides has visual continuity. No page reloads, no jarring state changes.
  • Confidence monitor — The presenter always knows what's next. The "upcoming slide" preview reduces anxiety.

What we should take:

  • The presenter view mental model — Organizer sees: current agenda item + sidebar with full agenda + notes panel. Projector shows: clean, focused content.
  • Click-to-advance simplicity — Our "Next" button should feel exactly like clicking to the next slide. Universally understood.
  • "What's next" preview — Show the next agenda item title in the sidebar so the organizer always knows what's coming.
  • Slide-like transitions — Smooth, fast transitions between agenda items. No full page reloads.

What to avoid:

  • PowerPoint is passive — slides don't have interactive elements. We need to enhance the "slide" with votes, galleries, document viewers.
  • The "blank slide" anxiety — if an agenda item has no content yet (e.g., no document uploaded), we need graceful handling, not an empty screen.

4. Mentimeter — Live Audience Interaction

What it does well:

  • Instant voting with live results — Ask a question, audience votes, results animate in real-time on the projector. The visual feedback is powerful.
  • Projector-first design — Every Mentimeter screen is designed to look great projected. Large fonts, high contrast, animated charts.
  • Zero friction voting — Participants go to menti.com, enter a code, vote. No account needed.
  • Results are the content — The vote visualization IS the presentation. Results appear as beautiful bar charts or word clouds.

What we should take:

  • Projector-optimized result display — When a vote completes, show the result large and clear: "Approved — 8/8 partners" with a visual bar or checkmarks. Make the result the hero of the screen.
  • Animation on result reveal — Mentimeter's animated bar charts building up create a moment of anticipation. A subtle animation when vote results appear adds delight.
  • The "no account needed" philosophy for stand-ins — Stand-ins don't need accounts. The organizer proxies. Same zero-friction spirit.

What to avoid:

  • Mentimeter requires individual devices — our partners don't interact on their own devices. The organizer is the sole operator.
  • Mentimeter's results can be noisy with large audiences — our 8-12 partners means results are simple and should stay simple (no word clouds, no scatter plots).

Flow Optimization Principles

  1. Minimize clicks during live meeting — Every agenda item interaction is 1-2 clicks max. No modals, no multi-step forms during presenter mode.
  2. Auto-populate everything possible — Attendance → minutes, vote results → minutes, snapshots → minutes. Organizer only adds what the system can't know (free-text notes).

  4. Async = same context, different timeline — Absent partner sees the same minutes, same documents, same vote results. Their actions (fee acceptance, feedback) feed back into the same system.
  5. Progressive readiness — Prep checklist turns from red → yellow → green as the organizer completes preparation. The "Start Presenter Mode" button only enables when minimum readiness is met.
