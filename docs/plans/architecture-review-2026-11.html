Sound context boundaries, one oversized context, and an API that has accreted

Verdict. The three contexts are cut correctly. CUMS owns identity, PCS owns partners, EMS owns the event. Behind the APIs, functionality is reused well in places, mail sending above all. Three things have drifted:

  • EMS has become the system: 74% of backend code, with no internal module boundaries.
  • The services call each other in both directions.
  • The OpenAPI surface has grown parallel endpoints for the same act: sending mail, uploading files, changing a task.

None of this is urgent. Most of it is cheap to fix now and expensive in a year.

1 · Who calls whom

Why it matters. A cycle means neither service can be deployed, tested or reasoned about alone. CUMS→EMS exists only because EMS must re-key its rows when two profiles merge. That is a fact CUMS can publish as an event, without CUMS ever knowing EMS exists. The trade-off is eventual consistency for a few seconds after a merge. For a merge organizers trigger by hand, that is acceptable.

2 · EMS is one context with ten sub-domains and no walls

Splitting EMS into more services would be the wrong move: every one of these modules reads the event. The right move is walls inside one deployable. Five packages already show the pattern works. Registration is the most tangled: 14 of its endpoints are hand-mapped inside EventController.

3 · Reuse behind the API: mail yes, uploads no

Reuse that did happen: attendee "topics from the floor" feed the same suggestion pool as partners. The Mail Center put every outbound mail in one log. Authorization lives in the services, with a guard test.

About 60–70 operations disappear if all of this is done. The figure is my estimate from the counts above, not a measurement. Two smaller conventions are worth a decision rather than a rewrite:

  • UUIDs in public paths. 61 operations put an internal ID in the path, against the letter of ADR-003. Amending ADR-003 to allow UUIDs for owned sub-resources (tasks, notes, photos) matches the practice. Recommended.
  • Literal segments under /events/. Six segments (types, current, registrations, recent-photos, …) share the namespace with {eventCode}. An event can never be called types.

5 · Recommendations, in order

  1. Delete dead surface. Two specs, six orphan endpoints, the dead fetchPreview hook. Low effort. Removes about 25 ops. Stopping the speaker-coordination ECS task is a production change, so it is your decision.
  2. Add a contract guard for frontend paths. Fail the build when a frontend service calls a path the spec doesn't have. This would have caught GET /publish/{phase}/preview, which exists in neither the spec nor the backend. Low effort. Stops the drift from coming back.
  3. Finish 16.8c. Fold newsletter, notice, venue and slides sends into the Mail Center endpoint. Already planned and gated on your go.
  4. Break the cycles. Service-to-service calls go through /internal only. Move the four machine-only endpoints out of the public spec. Replace CUMS→EMS with a UserMerged event. Medium effort. Costs a few seconds of eventual consistency after a merge.
  5. One upload pipeline, as ADR-002 already decided. Put a shared-kernel upload service behind /uploads and migrate one flow per release. Medium effort. 8 classes become 1.
  6. Wall EMS from the inside. Start with ArchUnit rules for the five vertical packages, then move registration out of EventController. Incremental, never a big bang.
  7. Settle the conventions. Amend ADR-003 for UUIDs, pick one speaker key, use one PATCH for tasks, one settings resource. Decide once, apply when touching each area.
